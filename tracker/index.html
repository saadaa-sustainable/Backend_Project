Quantity · units ordered by the hour

Shopify’s own quantity_ordered, grouped by number_of_products_bought_together — the hourly version of the Quantity report, with the same Return Prime channel excluded. Units here counts the gift coin; P&L units does not, and also drops inf and Return_Prime orders, so the two legitimately differ and neither corrects the other. Basket 0 is dropped: it is refund residue carrying negative sales and no orders, not something anyone bought. Rates are re-divided from the summed counts, never averaged across hours.

AOV is Shopify’s average_order_value: net sales before returns over orders. It is not Total sales ÷ orders, which reads a few percent low. Ret. rate is returning ÷ customers, Shopify’s own denominator — not returning ÷ (new + returning), because the two buckets overlap and sum higher than the customer count. Customers is a distinct count, so each hour is exact but the Day row sums anyone who ordered in two hours twice — about two percent over Shopify’s own day figure, moving the rate a quarter of a point. New customers sums exactly — nobody is new twice in a day. These are Shopify’s own classification and will not match the tracker’s new_customers, which is derived from each order’s lifetime count.

Coin Orders · Saadaa Silver Coin
Shipment standing
Dispatch desk · whole promotion Why a parcel is held, by the desk’s rules. A different question from the block above, over all days — the two are the same orders counted differently and are not meant to reconcile line for line.
Cost per coin is editable — the figures are re-queried on change, so they are always the ones the API computed, for the whole promotion rather than one day.

Coins and coin orders are different numbers: an order can ship more than one, so cost is taken on COINS while the attach rate is taken on ORDERS. Matched by SKU SDSILVER, not by title — the title carries an emoji that has moved three times.

Counted on the dispatch desk’s rules, so this section and the desk agree on what a coin order is. From 30 Oct 02:37 IST, when the free coin went live: earlier orders carry the same SKU but belong to the previous Saadaa Sagun offer. Cancelled, refunded and voided orders are left out, along with anything tagged Test Order and orders from internal accounts (@saadaa.in, @robylon.ai), plus the inf and Return_Prime tags the P&L also drops.

Already shipped and the payment split are read when each hour is ingested, and both move afterwards — a COD order becomes paid on delivery, an unfulfilled one ships. So a past day holds the state as it was then, not as it is now; re-ingest the campaign window to refresh them. Bundle, no coin is not a slice of the figures beside it: those orders carry no coin at all. The dispatch desk’s own Ship now, Call list and Address issues buckets stay there, because they depend on customer RTO history and full addresses this pipeline does not hold.

This is deliberately stricter than the P&L basis and the two should not be reconciled. The P&L keeps cancelled orders in and models cancellation as a rate, because it asks what the day earned. This asks how many coins we are liable for, and a cancelled order never shipped one.

Inventory · master → colour → variant
Virtual inventory
Source: Shopify Virtual (promised, never added to Shopify) Production sheet — background shows the source; red/amber/green shows whether the number is healthy.

Shopify only — every code is parsed out of the variant SKU (SMCCPBE_2XL → master SMCCP, colour BE, size 2XL). One quantity per SKU, ACTIVE listing preferred, the same rule CPIS uses. In stock is inventoryQuantity > 0, so oversold counts as out. Var in-stock % is variants holding stock over all variants. Size in-stock % is distinct sizes available in any colour — it catches size-run gaps the variant rate hides, and a product can be 31% on one and 100% on the other. Store-wide, the size rate is the MEAN of the per-product rates: taken literally across every product it would read 100% whenever the store sells each size somewhere.
Sheet comparison — the “OOS as per Production Plan” tab of the FY26-27 Production Sheet, at the same colour grain. Its in-stock % is shown as the sheet computes it, never recomputed: a difference in the rate is itself a finding. The two sides are snapshots of different moments — both stamps are in the header — so a small gap is a day’s trading, and only a gap over 5% of the sheet’s own figure is coloured. A dash means that SKU is absent from the sheet, which is a catalogue difference and not a stock discrepancy.
Virtual gap is Shopify minus virtual, shown only where virtual stock exists. Negative means more has been promised than Shopify can ship today.

Live P&L · day to date

Assumptions

The top line is Shopify's own Total sales, stored verbatim, so it equals what the Shopify admin shows, and the breakdown under it is Shopify’s own eight lines: gross less discounts less reversals is net sales, and plus shipping, return fees and taxes is the total. It is drawn only when both of those sums reach the headline exactly, so a window reaching back past the day those lines were first stored shows the headline alone rather than a breakdown that does not add up. Total sales still contains cancellations Shopify has not refunded — a COD order cancelled before payment produces no refund — and only that unrefunded part is deducted below, never the whole cancelled value. Everything under the Realisable sales line is a model built from the rates above, and the day is still running, so a margin here is an estimate rather than a result.
